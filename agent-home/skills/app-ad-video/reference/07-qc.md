# 07 · Quality control and presentation

Two separate jobs. **Measurement** is done with ffmpeg/ffprobe/face landmarks ([06](06-delivery.md)).
**Creative judgment** is done by a vision model and by eye. Do not hand one's job to the other.

```bash
SK=~/.claude/skills/app-ad-video/scripts
```

## Vision model QC (`fal analyze`, `fal ask`)

```bash
fal analyze Final/x.mp4 --out Work/qc/x.md --prompt "QC pass: does the hook land in the first 2 seconds? \
  Does the text carry the message with sound off? Is the CTA clear? List problems with timestamps."
fal analyze Final/x.mp4 --out Work/qc/x_vs_ref.md --prompt "Compare with the reference (Brief/reference-analysis.md \
  summarizes it). The only intended difference is the talent. List every other difference with timestamps: \
  text, timing, transitions, animation, audio."
fal ask "Is the caption fully inside the frame and readable?" --image Work/qc/f_030.png
fal analyze Final/x.mp4 --model google/gemini-2.5-pro ...     # a deeper model when it matters
```

- `fal analyze` sends the whole video (picture and sound) to a video-capable model; if that fails it falls back
  to contact sheets. Its output is a draft: verify anything numeric.
- Reliable: reading on-screen text, what is being said, the number and order of events, finding differences in a
  side-by-side comparison, transcripts.
- **Not reliable** (all of these came out wrong): pitch/frequency order (reported backwards), whether an audio
  event exists, whether text is out of frame, caption box color, packshot start time, levels, color values.
- Models sample frames sparsely; fast cuts and single-frame glitches slip through. Check those yourself.
- Comparing several audio takes: ask in one request with the files attached, or transcribe them and compare.

## Manual QC (always, and when no model is available)

1. Reference and ours side by side on the same frames:
   ```bash
   for f in 0 30 90 152 200 400; do python3 $SK/fx.py grab ref.mp4 $f Work/qc/r$f.png; python3 $SK/fx.py grab Final/x.mp4 $f Work/qc/b$f.png; done
   collage Work/qc/side.png 4 270 480 60 'Work/qc/r0.png|ref f0' 'Work/qc/b0.png|ours f0' …   # macOS helper
   ```
   Elsewhere, build the grid with ffmpeg `xstack`.
2. A contact sheet (`fx.py sheet Final/x.mp4 Work/qc/s --fps 3`); zoom in on transition frames.
3. OCR on key frames, `audio_match.py compare` for audio events, `fx.py cuts` for cuts.
4. In the report, state clearly which files were not reviewed by a model and why.

## Checklist (every master)

- Hook in the first 2 s; text inside `SAFE_AREA`; CTA on screen ≥ 1.5 s at the end; the message reads with sound
  off.
  - **Check `SAFE_AREA` by measurement, not by eye.** Grab a still with `stills.mjs`, draw the boundary on it,
    find the box's top edge by counting pure white pixels per row.
  - The glyph bound of outlined white text (the hook): pixels ≥ 248 white with < 70 dark within 4 px. A bright
    window in the shot does not give false positives. Once a hook glyph sat at y 207 with the bound at 210 and
    was moved 4 px down; it would not have been noticed by eye.
  - Compute the **peak scale** of overshoot animations too. A correct answer's 1.12 overshoot + a 6 px glow broke
    the bound by 1 px; it became 1.10.
  - A `cy` chosen for a single line overflows by half a line height on a two-line caption (26 to 38 px outside at
    9:16 in two cases).
  - The fix is to make the phone smaller and lower, or to merge lines in 4:5.
  - In typography sections, compute every line's top edge from the layout constants, especially lines added
    **above** a large element. Once a line above a big number overflowed to y 142 at 9:16 (bound 210) and y 70
    at 4:5 (bound 80). It was not visible in frames; the calculation found it. The fix was to shift the block
    down in that section.
- Transitions (±6 frames) are inspected at full resolution as a strip: do semi-transparent layers make what is
  behind them show through like a ghost?
- Face overlays sit right; the shot does not talk or look away; the slow-down or freeze is unnoticeable.
- Deliberate deviations from the reference (claim, palette, name) are applied correctly; no competitor name.
- The measurement audit ([06](06-delivery.md)) passed.
- **When verify says "saturated region difference > 3.5"**, separate measurement from delivery first, then touch
  the delivery (a small phone with a purple UI triggered it once).
  - `verify_delivery.py` decodes frames with the default `format=rgb24`. That conversion alone gives a systematic
    −3 / −1 / −4 shift on saturated purple.
  - Get three numbers:
    1. Reference PNG -> the same path (`scale=out_color_matrix=bt709:out_range=tv:flags=accurate_rnd+full_chroma_int,format=yuv420p`)
       raw yuv -> back **with verify's decoder** -> difference.
    2. The delivered file with verify's decoder (what verify reports).
    3. The delivered file with the correct decoder (`scale=in_color_matrix=bt709:in_range=tv:flags=accurate_rnd+full_chroma_int`).
  - Example: 3.43 / 3.72 / **1.96** (signed mean ~0). If 1 and 2 are close and 3 is ~2, the color path is right:
    the alarm comes from the measurement. A matrix error stays large in 3 too (a BT.601 error is ~6.8 on a CTA).
  - The same shift inflates the **whole-frame difference** too. On a very detailed frame (a street full of
    leaves) a lossless 4:2:0 frame already gives 2.12 to 2.14 with verify's decoder (−2.2 / −1.7 / −2.0).
    Encoding adds ~0.4 and it hits the limit at 2.50 to 2.56. With the correct decoder 1.67 to 1.82, unbiased.
    Separate it with the same three numbers.
  - **Exception: thin, contrasting text.** Yellow "30:00" on a purple chip (50 px at 9:16, 42 px at 4:5): a
    lossless 4:2:0 frame gives 3.43 / 4.35 even with the correct decoder (B +4.8 / +6.2: the purple bleeds into
    the yellow strokes); the delivery 4.45 / 5.73. That is 4:2:0 itself, and every platform converts to 4:2:0
    anyway. Compare by eye on a 3× crop (no visible difference). To avoid it, use white text or a dark stroke
    instead of yellow.
  - That is why the script's decoder was not changed. If it changes, the thresholds (2.5 / 3.5) must be measured
    again.
  - **Script:** `scripts/sat_diag.py <Final/x.mp4> <Work/ref_x/f_N.png> <N>` gives the three numbers and a fourth
    (lossless 4:2:0, correct decoder). The gap between 3 and 4 is the encoding's share.
    - Measured: a yellow karaoke word with black stroke 4.21 -> 5.21, a purple UI on a small phone 3.24 -> 4.17
      (9:16) and 4.70 -> 5.58 (4:5).
    - Encoding adds ~0.9 to 1.1 on thin edges, 0.2 to 0.4 on wide areas. No difference visible on a 3× crop.

## Presenting to the user

- **When a choice is needed:** a labeled collage (candidate images, existing talent, variants) with a short note
  under it. Ask with your recommendation. In one series 4 of 8 candidates were picked this way.
- **On delivery:** point to the files and write a short report. Report table: file, duration/frames, LUFS, peak,
  size. Next to it: what was verified and how, spend (`fal cost`, marked as estimated where it is), deliberate
  deviations and open items (for example "two masters were not reviewed by a model").
- Write the measurement table, the production pipeline, the cost breakdown and the lessons from mistakes into
  the creative's `PRODUCTION.md`. Keep a `HANDOFF.md` at the series root for the series' shared knowledge.
